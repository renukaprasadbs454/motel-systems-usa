1. Executive summary and product thesis

The prospect operates two franchise-affiliated motels in Fargo, North Dakota: Travelodge Fargo West Acres,
associated with Wyndham Hotels & Resorts, and Quality Suites, associated with Choice Hotels. The working
room-count assumptions are approximately 70 rooms and 50 rooms respectively (about 120 total); confirm exact
sellable inventory and room classes with the client.

The properties already use hotel reservation/property systems and pricing or revenue-management functionality.
The project is not to replace their franchise systems. Exora should sit above permitted source systems as a
decision-support and analytics layer.

The central build is an independent pricing recommendation engine. It will use the business signals that are
publicly documented in the incumbent systems' revenue-management descriptions--especially historical property
performance and projected occupancy--and extend that baseline with booking pace, inventory availability, rate
history, competitor data, event and weather context, channel economics and property-specific positioning where
data supports those features.

The platform should also consolidate daily/night operational and financial reports, because the client described a
manual management burden around ADR, cash flow, revenue excluding tax, discrepancies, no-shows,
cancellations and the hotel ledger. That reporting capability shares the same data foundation but is a distinct
product module from the pricing algorithm.

   Exora Hospitality Intelligence = a unified data platform + a hotel pricing decision engine + operations/financial analytics +

   explain validated calculations, not to invent financial numbers or independently control prices.

1.1 Business outcomes sought

 Recommend a room rate for each property, stay date and supported room class, with an explanation of why the rate is
     recommended.

 Forecast demand and occupancy early enough for managers to act on future dates, not just summarize yesterday.
 Compare each property against a relevant, normalized competitive set rather than comparing unqualified public prices.
 Bring both properties into one view while respecting the different franchise ecosystems and each property's own rules.
 Reduce the manager's manual effort reviewing daily/night reports and surface important exceptions.
 Allow a human manager to approve, edit or reject a recommendation before any production rate change.

3. What is documented about the incumbent revenue tools

  IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

   Vendor documentation describes product functions, not the complete proprietary feature vector, weights, coefficients, or
   optimization algorithm. Exora can adopt a compatible business-variable framework, but cannot claim to know or reproduce
   the private internal logic of ChoiceADVANTAGE or RevIQ from public descriptions.

3.1 ChoiceADVANTAGE -- publicly described baseline

Choice Hotels' 2025 Form 10-K says ChoiceADVANTAGE includes a rate and selling management tool that helps
franchisees manage rates and inventory to improve RevPAR by optimizing ADR and occupancy. It also describes
a revenue-management feature that calculates suggested optimum rates based on each hotel's past performance
and projected occupancy. [R2]

 Documented at a high level: property room inventory, rates, reservations, historical property performance and projected
     occupancy.

 Not disclosed in the public filing: the complete model input list, forecast method, feature weights, price elasticity method,
     competitor-rate feed details, exact update frequency or API schema for this property.

3.2 Wyndham RevIQ -- publicly described functions

A 2025 RevIQ supplement embedded in a La Quinta franchise disclosure document describes RevIQ Standard as
providing a daily optimization for base-price decisions and last-room value for the next 365 days, four intra-day
optimizations for the next 14 days, a daily 365-day occupancy forecast, non-base room-type pricing offsets, user-
configured special events and pricing seasons, seasonal price floors and ceilings, smart alerts, reporting and user-
configured competitive sets. The Premium tier adds up to three priced room classes and optimized hotel/room-type
overbooking. [R1]

Scope caveat: the source is a 2025 La Quinta franchise supplement, not the Travelodge owner's executed
agreement. It is useful to understand published RevIQ features, but the client's own Travelodge agreement,
configuration and RevIQ tier must be obtained before treating any term or feature as applicable.

5.4 Snapshot requirement -- non-negotiable

  WHY SNAPSHOTS MATTER

   A current reservations export tells us what is booked now. It does not tell us what was booked 30, 14 or 7 days before
   arrival. Without dated snapshots, we cannot reconstruct historical booking pace accurately. Start saving a daily snapshot of
   future occupancy, room rates and inventory for each property, stay date and room class from the first pilot day.

 Minimum daily snapshot horizon: next 60 days (30-day horizon is enough for the first demo, but 60 days gives more lead-
     time context).

 Retain raw reservation changes and snapshot timestamps; never overwrite prior snapshots with the latest totals.
 Capture whether each value is on-the-books, forecast, actual, or manually supplied. These must not be mixed.

7. Pricing recommendation methodology

7.1 Design principle

Do not ask an LLM to "guess the best rate." Build a deterministic and statistical pricing service that calculates
demand, evaluates price scenarios and returns a constrained recommendation. Use AI afterward to explain the
evidence in plain language.

7.2 Decision unit

The target decision key is: property × stay_date × room_class × rate_plan/condition (only where needed). For the
first MVP, start at property × stay_date × base room class; add additional priced room classes once the source
mapping and volume justify it.

7.3 Model pipeline

1. Create a clean daily panel for every property, stay date and room class, including capacity, OTB, actuals, current price and
     source freshness.

2. Build comparison features: same weekday, same season, same lead-time bucket, prior-year or comparable historical
     dates, booking pickup and cancellation pattern.

3. Forecast expected final demand/occupancy before pricing. Include uncertainty intervals and baseline benchmarks.

                                                                       EXORA | INTERNAL WORKING SPECIFICATION | PAGE 9
EXORA HOSPITALITY INTELLIGENCE | PRODUCT & ENGINEERING

4. Establish market context using normalized competitor rates and availability, plus event/weather indicators where data
     quality is sufficient.

5. Estimate how expected bookings respond to candidate prices. Do not assume the competitor median is the optimal price or
     that higher occupancy always means a price increase.

6. Optimize the candidate rate against expected net room revenue subject to property-specific price, inventory, rate-change
     and franchise constraints.

7. Create explanation codes, confidence, data-quality warnings and a transparent comparison to current/previous rate.
8. Require manager approval in the pilot; record all edits, rejections and override reasons.
9. Measure actual pickup, occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, net room revenue and forecast error after the stay date.

7.5 Objective function (conceptual)

  OPTIMIZATION TARGET

   For each stay date and room class, evaluate feasible candidate rate r and estimate expected sellable rooms Q(r). A simple
   objective is expected net room revenue = r × Q(r) × (1 - effective channel cost), with taxes/fees excluded from room
   revenue. Add operational constraints and, where data allows, incremental variable costs. This is a conceptual formulation;
   actual objective and cost treatment must be approved by the owner.

The engine must account for the fact that remaining rooms are finite. If occupancy is forecast to exceed capacity,
additional demand cannot create more room nights; the objective becomes selecting the best rate/mix under
capacity and overbooking rules. Overbooking should not be included in the first release unless the client explicitly
asks for it and the source data supports cancellations/no-shows accurately.

9.4 Data quality and reconciliation rules

 Every import must record property, source, upload time, business date, file hash, schema version, row counts and
     validation result.

 Deduplicate reservation updates using source reservation ID plus last-modified timestamp; preserve change history.
 Normalize all stay dates in the property timezone (America/Chicago for Fargo), while storing UTC timestamps for ingestion

     and events.
 Validate date ranges, currency, non-negative room counts, valid rate ranges, status values and room-class mapping.
 Reconcile nightly room revenue, tax, fees and payment totals with the source reports. Differences must be visible, not

     silently corrected.
 Keep raw CSVs and normalized records separately so mapping defects can be corrected and reprocessed.
 Flag missing snapshot dates; never backfill booking pace with guessed historical on-books counts.

11.1 Access and privacy

 Use property-level service accounts and role-based access; separate Travelodge and Quality Suites data by
     tenant/property permissions.

 Collect the minimum data needed for revenue analytics. Prefer pseudonymous reservation IDs; exclude guest names,
     email, phone, payment-card data and identity documents from the pricing dataset.

 Never ingest payment card numbers or security codes. Financial reporting should use totals, tender types and transaction
     references where approved, not cardholder data.

 Encrypt data in transit and at rest; store credentials/secrets in a secret manager; rotate API keys and maintain an access
     audit log.

 Apply retention and deletion schedules to raw reports, reservation records and external market data consistent with client
     contracts and vendor licensing.

 Use a data-processing agreement covering data ownership, purposes, sub-processors, retention, breach notice and
     derived data/model rights.

11.2 Pricing governance

 Each property maintains its own floor, ceiling, room-class relationships, blocked dates, rate-change limits and authorized
     approvers.

 The recommendation record stores model version, feature/data cutoff, inputs summary, constraints applied and reason
     codes.

 Manager approval is required for every live rate change in the pilot. No autonomous write-back until the owner,
     franchise/vendor and Exora security reviews approve it.

 Provide rollback to the last approved rate and an exportable audit history.
 Avoid personalized pricing based on individual guest characteristics. Keep the decision at property/date/room-class level.
